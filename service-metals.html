Metals department

Metal manufacturing

From design and tooling through cutting, forming, welding, and finishing — a complete metals process chain with capacity for long parts, production volume, and staged inventory.

Overview

Cut. Form. Finish.

Our metals division pairs in-house tooling with production cells built for real manufacturing schedules. We carry substantial steel inventory, run overhead cranes up to 20 tons, and keep space to store and ship as you need it.

  • Fiber & CO₂ laser cutting

    High-powered lasers cut sheet and plate with precision and clean edges. Fiber lasers excel on reflective and thinner metals; CO₂ lasers remain strong for a range of materials and thicknesses. Ideal for prototypes through production nests.

  • Turret punch & combo laser

    Turret punching forms holes, louvers, and repeated features quickly in sheet metal. Combo laser/punch systems let us cut complex profiles and punch features in one setup — faster throughput with fewer handling steps.

  • Press brakes up to 750 tons

    Press brakes bend flat blanks into flanges, channels, and formed shapes. High tonnage capacity supports thick material and long parts that smaller shops cannot form in-house.

  • OBI presses to 165 tons

    Open-back inclinable (OBI) presses stamp and form metal using dies. They are workhorses for progressive and secondary operations where consistent, repeatable force is required.

  • Metal stamping

    Stamping uses dies and presses to blank, pierce, form, or draw sheet metal into finished or near-net shapes. Best for repeatable parts where tooling investment pays back over volume.

  • Roll forming (2" & 3" lines)

    Roll forming progressively shapes long metal strip through a series of rolls into continuous profiles (channels, angles, custom sections). Efficient for long, uniform parts that would be hard to press-brake piece by piece.

  • CNC machining centers

    Computer-controlled mills machine precise features, pockets, holes, and surfaces in metal billets or fabricated blanks. CNC delivers tight tolerances and repeatable geometry for fixtures, components, and secondary ops.

  • TIG, MIG & robotic welding

    TIG offers precise, clean welds on thinner or critical joints; MIG is fast and strong for production fabrication. Robotic welding adds consistency and speed for repeat weld paths on higher-volume assemblies.

  • Automated tank seam welding

    Specialized seam welding joins long, continuous joints on tanks and cylindrical assemblies with uniform quality — reducing distortion and labor compared with hand-welding long seams.

  • Powder coating

    A dry powder finish electrostatically applied and cured into a durable, uniform coating. Provides corrosion resistance and color options without the solvents of traditional wet paint.

  • Tool & die, jigs & fixtures

    Custom tooling that enables stamping, forming, and welding operations. Dies create the part shape; jigs and fixtures hold workpieces accurately so production stays consistent.

  • Graphics printing

    Applied markings, logos, and product graphics for finished metal or plastic goods — useful for branding, instructions, and product identification on manufactured parts.
