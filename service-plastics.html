Plastics manufacturing

Idea to finished part — 3D design, in-house mold building, multi-cell molding, and robotic finishing for strong, durable plastic components in many shapes, sizes, and colors.

Overview

Mold. Finish. Ship.

Seljan’s plastics division grew out of decades of mold-making experience. We run rotational, injection, and blow molding with tooling built on-site — so design changes and production timelines stay under one roof.

  • Rotational molding

    Also called rotomolding: powdered plastic is placed in a hollow mold that rotates biaxially while heated, coating the interior evenly. After cooling, a seamless hollow part is removed. Excellent for large, durable, hollow products.

  • Injection molding

    Molten plastic is injected under pressure into a closed mold cavity, then cooled and ejected as a solid part. Suited to detailed geometries and both short-run and high-volume production. See our dedicated injection molding page for press details.

  • Blow molding

    A heated plastic parison (tube) is inflated inside a mold so it takes the cavity shape — commonly used for hollow bottles, containers, and similar forms with relatively uniform wall thickness.

  • 9 rotomolding work cells

    Independent production cells let us run multiple molds and products in parallel. More cells mean higher capacity, better scheduling flexibility, and the ability to keep long-running programs moving 24/6.

  • Robotic finishing cells

    Robots trim, drill, rout, or otherwise finish molded parts with consistent paths and cycle times. Reduces manual labor variation and speeds secondary operations after molding.

  • Custom colors & textures

    Plastic resins can be pigmented and surface finishes controlled through mold texture and process settings — matching brand colors or functional surface requirements (grip, matte, gloss).

  • Large hollow parts

    Rotomolding’s strength is scale: tanks, housings, bins, and other sizable hollow structures that would be costly or impossible as single-piece injection moldings.

  • Material handling products

    Industrial bins, containers, and related handling goods molded for durability in warehouse and plant environments — including custom sizes and lid styles when needed.

  • Urethane casting options

    Pouring urethane into molds to create prototype or low-volume parts that mimic production plastics. Useful for fit/function checks before committing to production tooling.

  • In-house mold department

    We design and machine molds on-site. Keeping tooling in-house shortens lead times (often 4–5 weeks to production-ready tools) and makes revisions faster when designs evolve.
